Background

I came out of NC State in 2025 with two degrees — computer science and applied mathematics — a 4.0, and a Park Scholarship, the full ride NC State gives to about the top 1% of applicants for leadership and service rather than grades alone. The maths half is not decoration: it is why a batch ledger that has to reconcile to the cent every night feels like a natural place to work.

The longer thread is teaching. I joined InspireNC at seventeen — a community non-profit in North Carolina's Triangle whose stated aim is to promote interest, knowledge and involvement in STEAM and to build leadership in the people who come through it. I started as a mechanical designer and programmer on FRC Team 6908, “Infuzed”, its FIRST Robotics Competition team for 9th–12th graders. I became the team's Robotics Representative, and then the organisation's Chairman — a role I held for five years, until I graduated. Eight years with one organisation, most of them spent getting other people started.

In practice that is free bootcamps and weekend workshops for high-schoolers who would not otherwise get near a robot, teaching Java to cohorts of 35 at a time, running outreach across the Triangle, and keeping a competitive FRC team funded and building through a season. The programme has reached 100+ students. Alongside it I was a teaching assistant for Advanced MATLAB in NC State's CS department, where I answered over 1,000 questions on the course forum and rebuilt the assignment set around what students were actually getting stuck on.

I have been teaching people to program for almost as long as I have been programming. It is the part of the work I would keep if I had to give up the rest.

It shows up in how I build. The write-ups on this site are the same instinct — explain the decision, name the thing that nearly went wrong, say what I would do differently. A system nobody else can reason about is a system with one point of failure, and it is usually me.
